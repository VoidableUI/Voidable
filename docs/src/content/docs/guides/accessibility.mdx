Components are built on the platform: native controls where there is one, ARIA roles and states where there is not, and keyboard handling for every interactive widget. This page says what you get, what you still have to provide, and where it falls short today. The [accessibility statement](/guides/accessibility-statement/) says what the project aims for, what is tested and how (including scripted VoiceOver and NVDA runs), and what is not.

## What you get

- **Native controls underneath.** `<void-input>`, `<void-textarea>`, `<void-slider>`, `<void-checkbox>` and `<void-radio>` render a real `<input>` or `<textarea>`, and `<void-select>` enhances the real `<select>` you write inside it. Focus, selection, autofill, the on-screen keyboard and screen-reader behaviour are the browser's.
- **Roles and states on the element.** A `<void-switch>` is `role="switch"` with `aria-checked`, a `<void-dialog>` renders a native `<dialog>` opened with `showModal()` (the browser exposes it as a modal dialog and makes the page behind it inert), a `<void-progress>` is `role="progressbar"` with `aria-valuenow`. The role, name source and state handles of every component are in the table below and on its page.
- **Keyboard support.** Interactive components handle the keys their pattern calls for. The keys are listed on the [keyboard page](/components/keyboard/) and in each component's page.
- **Labels and errors wired for you.** A `<void-field>` names the control inside it (`for` on a native control, `aria-labelledby` otherwise), describes it with the helper text, and while `error` is set marks it `aria-invalid` and describes it with the message.
- **Light DOM.** Assistive technology and browser extensions see ordinary elements; there is no shadow boundary for `aria-labelledby` or `for` to fail to cross. Only the dialog and the drawer have a shadow root, and their title and description stay in the light DOM, linked by ARIA element references.
- **Motion and colour scheme follow the user's preferences.** The theme reads `prefers-reduced-motion` and `prefers-color-scheme` in its tokens.

## What you still have to provide

A component cannot know what your page means. These are yours:

- **An accessible name for anything that has no text.** Give an icon-only `<void-button>` an `aria-label`, a chart an `aria-label` and the key figures in text beside it, a `<void-carousel>` and a `<void-menu>` an `aria-label`, and a `<void-panel>` a `label`. A control without a visible label goes in a `<void-field label="…">`.
- **Meaningful headings and landmarks.** A card's `heading` is styled text, not an `<h2>`; use your own heading element where the page outline needs one.
- **Live status where you need it.** Loading, saving and error messages that appear without focus moving should be announced: the [toast container](/components/toast-container/) announces each toast through hidden live announcers, and [alert](/components/alert/) is a live region; a skeleton is not.
- **Focus management around your own flows.** Overlays manage focus themselves, see [Overlays](/components/overlays/). An open dialog or drawer moves focus to its `autofocus` element, else its first focusable element, keeps Tab inside it (the page behind is inert), closes on Escape (unless `closable="false"`) and returns focus to the control that opened it. A popover, menu or picker list takes focus when a user opens it, closes on Escape or when focus leaves, and returns focus to its trigger. Where focus goes after your own actions (a deleted row, a submitted form) is up to your code. Name every dialog and drawer with `heading`, or `aria-label` when there is no visible title.

## Right-to-left

Set `dir="rtl"` on `<html>` (or on any container) and the theme lays the components out from the right. The theme's CSS uses logical properties, so these follow the writing direction:

- Spacing, borders, corner radii and edge offsets: the number input's prefix, unit and stepper buttons, the action input's button, select arrows, the sidebar's rule, the close button of an alert, toast, dialog or drawer, the shortcut in a menu item, and the date picker's icon.
- Text alignment of table headers, accordion and collapsible triggers, and the slider's value.
- Flow: tabs, breadcrumbs, pagination, radio and checkbox groups, and the order of a row of buttons.
- Direction of motion and fades: the switch thumb, the carousel slide, the indeterminate progress bar, the slider fill, the scroll fades of tabs, tables and navigation, and the accordion chevron.

Arrow keys follow the direction too (the test is `:dir(rtl)` on the component, so `dir` and `lang` count). In right-to-left, Left Arrow moves to the next item and Right Arrow to the previous in tabs, toggle group, segmented control, radio group, carousel, nav menu, the calendar and date picker day grid and the PIN input. Up Arrow, Down Arrow, Home, End, Page Up and Page Down do not swap. The slider is a native input, so the browser applies its own direction rule to it. The colour picker's saturation area is a physical two-dimensional surface and its keys do not swap.

What does not follow yet:

- Built-in strings and glyphs: the pagination `«` and `»`, the breadcrumb separator you supply and the default `→` of `void-delta` are written text and do not mirror. Labels the components generate are in English.
- Attributes that name a physical place keep their meaning (on a popover, tooltip, hover card or dropdown menu, `side="left"` and `"right"` mirror with the direction): drawer `side="left" | "right"` (the screen edges; `start` and `end` follow the direction), toast `placement`, indicator `placement`, and the color picker (its hue and saturation axes are a colour scale, not text). The sidebar `side` and the action input `icon-side` take `start` and `end`, which follow the direction.
- Locale: the calendar, date picker and number input do not yet take a locale, and a page that mixes scripts needs `dir` on the right element.

## Checking your own pages

- Run the [development warnings](/getting-started/development-warnings/) in development: they catch misused attributes and missing children.
- Run `voidable check` over your templates in CI.
- Run an automated checker such as axe-core against the rendered page. The project's [agent evals](/getting-started/agent-evals/) do, and count any serious violation against the generated page.
- Use the page with a keyboard alone, and with a screen reader, before you ship a new flow. Automated checks find a fraction of the problems; the [statement](/guides/accessibility-statement/) lists what the project's own checks cover and what they cannot.
