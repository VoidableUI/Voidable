`<void-toolbar>` is the row of controls above a table or a list: a search field, filters, a count, then actions at the far end. It gives the search field room, keeps the filters compact, and wraps when it runs out of width.

export const toolbar = `
<void-toolbar>
  <void-input type="search" placeholder="Search stations" aria-label="Search stations"></void-input>
  <void-select aria-label="Station region">
    <select>
      <option>All regions</option>
      <option>Arctic</option>
      <option>Southern</option>
    </select>
  </void-select>
  <void-select aria-label="Station state">
    <select>
      <option>Any state</option>
      <option>Online</option>
      <option>Degraded</option>
    </select>
  </void-select>
  <void-spacer></void-spacer>
  <span>44 stations</span>
  <void-button variant="ghost">Export</void-button>
</void-toolbar>`;

## Order

Left to right, from what narrows the list to what acts on it:

1. **Search**, first, because it is used most.
2. **Filters**: selects, a segmented control, a multiselect.
3. **`<void-spacer>`**: everything after it sits at the far end.
4. **A count** in a `<span>`: it is set small and quiet, in tabular figures.
5. **Actions on the list**: `ghost` buttons. The page's primary action belongs in the [page head](/patterns/page-head/), not here.

Every control needs a name. There is no room for visible labels in a toolbar, so give each field an `aria-label`; the placeholder is not a label.

## With a table

Put the toolbar and the table in one section. The toolbar has no line of its own: the table's header line closes it.

export const withTable = `
<void-section>
  <void-toolbar>
    <void-input type="search" placeholder="Search stations" aria-label="Search stations"></void-input>
    <void-spacer></void-spacer>
    <span>3 of 44</span>
  </void-toolbar>
  <void-table aria-label="Stations">
    <table>
      <thead><tr><th>Station</th><th>State</th><th data-numeric>Passes</th><th></th></tr></thead>
      <tbody>
        <tr>
          <td><void-row-text><span>Svalbard 1</span><span data-void-type="caption">Arctic</span></void-row-text></td>
          <td><void-state color="success">Online</void-state></td>
          <td data-numeric>212</td>
          <td><void-actions justify="end"><void-button variant="ghost" size="sm">Open</void-button></void-actions></td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
          <td><void-row-text><span>Tromsø 2</span><span data-void-type="caption">Arctic</span></void-row-text></td>
          <td><void-state color="warning">Degraded</void-state></td>
          <td data-numeric>96</td>
          <td><void-actions justify="end"><void-button variant="ghost" size="sm">Open</void-button></void-actions></td>
        </tr>
      </tbody>
    </table>
  </void-table>
</void-section>`;

<Example html={withTable} />

In a table, `data-numeric` on a `<th>` and its `<td>`s sets a column of numbers at the end of the cell in tabular figures, `void-row-text` gives a cell two lines, and `void-actions` in the last cell holds the row's `ghost` buttons. On a narrow screen, drop the columns that matter least with `data-void-hide="narrow"` on their header and cells.

## On a narrow screen

The row wraps when it runs out of width: the search field keeps at least 12rem and the rest flows onto the next line. A toolbar with more than three filters should move them into a `void-popover` behind one "Filters" button.

## Why this form

A toolbar is CSS only: a wrapping flex row that knows which of its children are fields. It is not a component because it has no state of its own; the search and the filters belong to your form, your framework or your server.
